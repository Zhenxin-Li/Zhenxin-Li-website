Low P/E Is a Starting Point, Not an Institutional Endorsement

What a small, archived Finviz scrape can and cannot tell a researcher

web-scraping
data-analysis
Author

Zhenxin Li

Published

September 20, 2026

A low price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio can make a stock look like a bargain. But does a low P/E also mean institutions hold a large share of the company? If so, investors might treat the two measures as reinforcing signals. If not, a low P/E should be a starting point for further investigation, not an endorsement.

Using a preserved historical Finviz sample, I compare P/E ratios and reported institutional ownership. The answer is not consistently: among the six companies with P/E at most 10, ownership ranges from 20.64% to 98.61%. The useful decision is therefore not which stock to buy, but which questions to ask next.

Do low-P/E companies also have high institutional ownership?

The figure compares P/E and ownership within the same companies. The dashed line marks a P/E of 10, a simple screening threshold rather than a definition of fair value. The dotted line marks the sample’s median institutional ownership of 70.91%. The horizontal P/E axis is logarithmic so that both lower and higher multiples remain visible.

Only 6 of 22 companies pass the P/E screen. Among those six, three have ownership below the sample median and three are at or above it. That split challenges the idea that inexpensive-looking stocks uniformly attract high institutional ownership. It does not establish that institutions avoid low-P/E stocks: ownership levels are not changes in holdings, and the sample is too small and selective for a market-wide conclusion.

Which inexpensive-looking companies deserve a closer look?

The table makes the difference concrete. These are research candidates, not buy recommendations.

Consider Array Digital Infrastructure, with P/E 4.13 and institutional ownership 20.64%, alongside ADT, with P/E 9.17 and ownership 98.61%. Both satisfy the same valuation screen, yet their reported ownership profiles differ sharply. The sensible next step is to examine why: are reported earnings recurring, is debt manageable, and do the firms have different business or shareholder structures? None of those questions can be answered from P/E and ownership alone.

For a researcher with limited time, I would prioritize the three low-P/E, below-median-ownership companies for additional ownership and earnings checks, while keeping the other three on the same due-diligence list. This is a triage rule, not evidence that one group will perform better.

A tighter ceiling of 8 yields 5 companies, while a ceiling of 12 yields 9. The candidate list therefore depends on a discretionary threshold. A screen can organize research efficiently, but it should not be mistaken for a stable classification of undervalued firms.

What the evidence cannot tell us

The analysis uses a preserved Finviz CSV assembled through an original rvest scraper, not a fresh download. After removing exact duplicates and excluding ETFs, shell companies, non-U.S. classifications, firms outside the $300 million to below $10 billion market-cap range, invalid P/E values, and missing or out-of-range ownership figures, 22 companies remain. The source is an alphabetical convenience sample, not a representative sample of U.S. equities. The original HTML and request log were not preserved, so the exact observation date and original field mappings cannot be independently verified.

A second limitation is that vendor-reported ownership above 100% appears in the raw data. These observations are excluded from the main comparison, rather than silently clipped; the README reports the sensitivity check retaining them. The historical rvest collector is retained for documentation, not rerun, because current Finviz screener access rules restrict generic automated requests. No login, CAPTCHA or other access control is bypassed.

Takeaway

Low P/E is a useful filter, not an institutional endorsement. In this sample, companies meeting the same low-P/E criterion have very different ownership levels. For a practical next step, use the screen to build a short research queue, then verify earnings quality, leverage, business risks and ownership disclosures before drawing investment conclusions. The evidence supports a research workflow, not a claim about future stock returns.

# Reanalyse the preserved historical rvest output; ordinary rendering is offline.
library(dplyr)
library(ggplot2)
raw_dir <- file.path(post_dir,"data","raw")
results_dir <- file.path(post_dir,"results")
fig_dir <- file.path(post_dir,"figures")
dir.create(results_dir,recursive=TRUE,showWarnings=FALSE)
dir.create(fig_dir,recursive=TRUE,showWarnings=FALSE)
raw <- read.csv(file.path(raw_dir,"finviz_original.csv"),stringsAsFactors=FALSE)
required <- c("ticker","company_name","sector","industry","country","market_cap","pe","inst_own","short_float")
stopifnot(all(required %in% names(raw)))
num <- function(x) suppressWarnings(as.numeric(gsub("[%x,]","",x)))
cap <- function(x) {
  v <- suppressWarnings(as.numeric(sub("[BMT]$","",x)))
  v * ifelse(grepl("T$",x),1e12,ifelse(grepl("B$",x),1e9,ifelse(grepl("M$",x),1e6,NA_real_)))
}
unique_rows <- distinct(raw)
stopifnot(!anyDuplicated(unique_rows$company_name))
parsed <- unique_rows |>
  rename(source_row_number=ticker) |>
  mutate(pe_num=num(pe),inst_pct=num(inst_own),cap_usd=cap(market_cap))
us <- parsed |> filter(country=="USA",!grepl("Exchange Traded Fund|Shell Companies",industry))
sized <- us |> filter(is.finite(cap_usd),cap_usd>=300e6,cap_usd<10e9)
earning <- sized |> filter(is.finite(pe_num),pe_num>0,pe_num<=200)
complete <- earning |> filter(is.finite(inst_pct),inst_pct>=0)
df <- complete |> filter(inst_pct<=100)
stopifnot(nrow(df)>0)
inst_cut <- median(df$inst_pct)
df <- df |> mutate(low_pe=pe_num<=10,lower_inst=inst_pct<inst_cut,
                   review_flag=low_pe & lower_inst)
audit <- data.frame(stage=c("Original merged rows","Exact duplicate rows removed","Unique companies",
 "USA companies excluding ETFs and shells","Market cap 300m to below 10bn",
 "Positive P/E at most 200","Nonmissing nonnegative institutional ownership","Ownership at most 100%"),
 n=c(nrow(raw),nrow(raw)-nrow(unique_rows),nrow(unique_rows),nrow(us),nrow(sized),nrow(earning),nrow(complete),nrow(df)))
write.csv(audit,file.path(results_dir,"sample_flow.csv"),row.names=FALSE)
write.csv(df |> select(source_row_number,company_name,sector,industry,country,cap_usd,pe_num,inst_pct,low_pe,lower_inst,review_flag),
 file.path(results_dir,"clean_companies.csv"),row.names=FALSE)
shortlist <- df |> filter(low_pe) |> arrange(pe_num) |>
 select(company_name,sector,pe_num,inst_pct,lower_inst)
write.csv(shortlist,file.path(results_dir,"low_pe_companies.csv"),row.names=FALSE)
sensitivity <- bind_rows(lapply(c(8,10,12),function(cutoff) data.frame(pe_cutoff=cutoff,
 low_pe_n=sum(df$pe_num<=cutoff),below_median_ownership_n=sum(df$pe_num<=cutoff & df$lower_inst))))
write.csv(sensitivity,file.path(results_dir,"threshold_sensitivity.csv"),row.names=FALSE)
wide_inst <- median(complete$inst_pct)
write.csv(data.frame(sample=c("Main: ownership <=100%","Sensitivity: retain >100% reports"),
 n=c(nrow(df),nrow(complete)),inst_median=c(inst_cut,wide_inst),
 low_pe_n=c(sum(df$low_pe),sum(complete$pe_num<=10)),
 below_median_ownership_n=c(sum(df$review_flag),sum(complete$pe_num<=10 & complete$inst_pct<wide_inst))),
 file.path(results_dir,"ownership_sensitivity.csv"),row.names=FALSE)
thresholds <- data.frame(pe_cutoff=10,inst_median_pct=inst_cut,n=nrow(df),low_pe_n=sum(df$low_pe),
 below_median_ownership_n=sum(df$review_flag))
write.csv(thresholds,file.path(results_dir,"thresholds.csv"),row.names=FALSE)
checks <- data.frame(check=c("Unique company records","Source IDs identified as row numbers", "USA only",
 "Explicit market cap range", "Complete analysis fields", "Low PE subset follows declared rule"),
 passed=c(!anyDuplicated(df$company_name),all(grepl("^[0-9]+$",raw$ticker)),all(df$country=="USA"),
 all(df$cap_usd>=300e6 & df$cap_usd<10e9),all(is.finite(as.matrix(df[c("pe_num","inst_pct")]))),
 all(shortlist$pe_num<=10)))
stopifnot(all(checks$passed))
write.csv(checks,file.path(results_dir,"validation.csv"),row.names=FALSE)
write.csv(data.frame(file="finviz_original.csv",md5=unname(tools::md5sum(file.path(raw_dir,"finviz_original.csv")))),
 file.path(results_dir,"snapshot_checksum.csv"),row.names=FALSE)
writeLines(capture.output(sessionInfo()),file.path(results_dir,"sessionInfo.txt"))
theme_set(theme_minimal(base_size=12))
fig1 <- ggplot(df,aes(pe_num,inst_pct)) +
 geom_point(aes(colour=low_pe),size=3,alpha=.85) +
 geom_vline(xintercept=10,linetype="dashed",colour="#334155") +
 geom_hline(yintercept=inst_cut,linetype="dotted",colour="#334155") +
 scale_x_log10(breaks=c(5,10,20,50,100,200)) +
 scale_colour_manual(values=c("FALSE"="#176b91","TRUE"="#b45309"),labels=c("P/E above 10","P/E at most 10")) +
 labs(title="Low P/E companies do not have identical ownership",subtitle=paste0(nrow(df)," historical sample companies; each point is one company"),
 x="Trailing P/E (logarithmic scale)",y="Reported institutional ownership (%)",colour=NULL,
 caption="Dashed: P/E = 10. Dotted: sample median ownership. Source: preserved Finviz scrape; capture date unverified.") +
 theme(legend.position="bottom",plot.caption=element_text(size=9))
ggsave(file.path(fig_dir,"company_screen.png"),fig1,width=10,height=6,dpi=180)
