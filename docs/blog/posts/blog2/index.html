Low P/E Is a Starting Point, Not an Institutional Endorsement

What a small, archived Finviz scrape can and cannot tell a researcher

web-scraping
data-analysis
Author

Zhenxin Li

Published

September 20, 2026

A low price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio means a company’s share price is low relative to its reported earnings. Investors often use it to narrow a list of stocks to investigate. They may also look at institutional ownership—the percentage of shares reported as held by institutions such as investment funds. Do companies that pass a low-P/E screen also have above-median institutional ownership?

I examine this question using 22 companies in an archived Finviz dataset. I define the low-P/E group as companies with positive trailing P/E ratios of 10 or less and compare their ownership percentages with the median in this sample. The threshold is a convenient screen, not a determination of fair value.

Do low-P/E stocks have high institutional ownership?

Each point in Figure 1 is a company. The vertical dashed line marks P/E = 10; the horizontal dotted line marks median institutional ownership of 70.91%. The P/E axis is logarithmic.

6 of 22 companies meet the low-P/E screen. Their reported institutional ownership ranges from 20.64% to 98.61%. Three fall below the sample ownership median and three are above it. In this sample, passing the same P/E screen does not consistently mean having above-median institutional ownership.

What should an investor examine next?

Table 1 identifies the six companies behind the highlighted points.

Array Digital Infrastructure has a P/E of 4.13 and reported institutional ownership of 20.64%. ADT also passes the screen, with a P/E of 9.17, but its reported ownership is 98.61%—a difference of 77.97 percentage points. These are two different ownership profiles among companies selected by the same P/E rule.

For a practical stock-research workflow, use P/E to identify candidates, then check institutional ownership as a separate piece of information. Before narrowing the list further, examine each company’s financial filings for recurring versus unusual earnings, debt obligations, and updated ownership disclosures. These are follow-up checks, not explanations established by this dataset.

Data and scope

The analysis uses an archived Finviz CSV associated with a historical rvest script. The cleaned dataset contains 22 U.S.-classified companies after excluding ETFs and shell companies, restricting market capitalization to $300 million–below $10 billion, and retaining positive P/E ratios up to 200 and institutional ownership from 0% to 100%. It is a limited alphabetical sample, so the comparisons describe these 22 companies.

The original HTML and request log were not retained, so the historical extraction cannot be independently verified. Rendering reads the saved CSV without sending requests to Finviz. The README documents the processing rules and source limitations.

Takeaway

Low P/E is a starting point, not an institutional endorsement. Among these six low-P/E companies, institutional ownership varies from about 21% to 99%. A low-P/E screen can produce a manageable research list, but investors should check ownership and company filings separately before deciding which companies warrant closer attention.

Source: archived Finviz screener data (collection date unverified). Finviz definitions; access rules.
